AI camera movement prompts: 12 moves, and how to make the AI actually follow them

Every AI video model understands “slow push-in” or “orbit around the subject”. Getting the exact move, at the right moment, without the camera wandering off, is the hard part. Here are prompts that work, why they drift, and what to do when words aren’t enough.

By the CineHand team · Updated 9 November 2026 · Tested on Seedance 2.5

In short
  • Write the camera as its own sentence: move, direction, speed, and what stays fixed.
  • One move per shot. Combined moves (“walk in, glance left, whip pan”) are where prompts fail.
  • End with “no other camera movement”. Models love to add a move back to the main subject.
  • Words can’t set timing. When the shot needs an exact path, give the model a video of the move instead. You can shoot one with your phone in CineHand.

The camera prompt formula

Video models read a prompt as a bag of ideas. If the camera words are mixed into the description of the scene, the model weighs them against everything else and often loses them. Keep them apart:

[shot size + subject] + [camera move, direction, speed] + [what stays fixed] + [look]

Medium shot of a man standing between two parked cars at night. The camera slowly pushes in toward his face over the whole shot. He stays still and keeps looking at the lens. No other camera movement. Wet asphalt, sodium street light, shot on an old phone.

Three habits matter more than the exact wording:

  • Name the move with a film term (push-in, pan, orbit, crane). Models were trained on captions that use these words.
  • Give a speed word: slowly, steadily, quickly, a sudden whip. Without one, most models default to a slow, smooth move.
  • Say what doesn’t move. “The subject stays still”, “the horizon stays level”, “the camera does not return to the man”.

Notes for Seedance, Kling, Veo and Runway

Seedance 2.5 (tested)

We run every camera move through Seedance 2.5 with a phone-shot motion reference and score the result frame by frame. What we’ve seen so far:

  • With a motion reference, timing holds. In good runs the camera hit its turns within about 0.05 s of the reference, and natural hand shake survived (0.32° of wobble in the take, 0.38° in the video).
  • It likes to return to the main subject. When the move turns away from a person, some runs swing back to them at the end. Say explicitly that the camera ends pointing away and the subject can leave the frame.
  • It slightly under-walks. Forward movement came out at roughly 75–93% of the distance in the reference.
  • Not every run follows. About two in three runs followed the move closely in our early tests, so make two or three versions and keep the closest.

Kling, Veo, Runway and others

Kling, Runway and Higgsfield offer preset camera moves (zoom, pan, orbit and similar) that are more reliable than free text for simple moves. Veo and Sora rely mostly on prompt wording. We haven’t run our move tests on these models yet, so we won’t claim more than that: the formula above is a good default for all of them, and a video reference is the strongest control wherever a model accepts one.

Why the camera drifts

A prompt describes a category of move. A shot needs a performance: this speed, this pause, turn here, overshoot a little, settle. Text can’t carry that, so the model fills the gaps with the most common version it has seen, which is a smooth, slow, centred move. Three things follow from that:

  • Timing is guessed. “Halfway through, whip pan left” lands anywhere from a third to two thirds of the way in.
  • Combined moves collapse. “Walk in, glance left, whip pan” usually becomes one blended move.
  • The camera returns to safety. Models are biased toward keeping the main subject in frame.

FAQ

What is a camera movement prompt?

A sentence in your AI video prompt that tells the model how the virtual camera should move: the move (push-in, pan, orbit), its direction, its speed and what stays fixed. Most video models, including Seedance, Kling and Veo, understand common film terms.

How do I stop the AI from adding camera movement I didn't ask for?

Ask for one move per shot, say what must stay still (“the camera does not pan back to the man”), and end the prompt with “no other camera movement”. If the shot needs an exact path, give the model a video reference of the move instead of words.

Can I control the timing of a camera move with a prompt?

Only roughly. Words like “slowly” or “halfway through” are interpreted loosely. To land a turn at a specific moment, use a camera-motion reference video. In our Seedance 2.5 tests, a reference shot with a phone kept the timing within about 0.05 seconds.

Which camera moves work best in AI video?

Slow push-ins, slow pull-outs, pans and orbits around a still subject are the most reliable from text alone. Whip pans, handheld moves and combined moves (walk in, glance, whip) are where prompts drift most, and where a motion reference helps most.

Can I use my own camera move as a reference for AI video?

Yes. Models that accept a video reference can follow the camera path in it. CineHand lets you shoot that reference with your phone through a 3D blockout of your scene, then makes the AI video and scores how closely it followed.
